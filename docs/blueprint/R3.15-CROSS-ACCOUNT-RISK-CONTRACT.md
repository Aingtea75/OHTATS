# OHTATS R3.15 — Cross-Account Risk Contract

**Status:** REVIEW

**Canonical Authority:** Risk Management (`RISK_MANAGEMENT.md`)

**Scope:** Normative sub-contract for cross-account exposure. This document does not create a second risk authority and does not authorize runtime implementation by itself.

## 1. Purpose

Cross-account exposure exists when multiple trading accounts can contribute to a shared risk scope such as user, tenant, portfolio, strategy/deployment, instrument, broker, or platform exposure.

The aggregate must be deterministic, auditable, and decomposable to contributing account-level state.

## 2. Account-Set Resolution

Every cross-account evaluation resolves an explicit account set using stable account identifiers and an applicable policy scope.

Possible scopes include:

- all active accounts of a user;
- all accounts in a tenant;
- accounts explicitly bound to a portfolio;
- accounts in a strategy/deployment scope;
- accounts sharing a governed instrument, broker, or platform exposure scope.

Accounts outside the resolved scope must not silently contribute.

The account set and scope rule are part of the decision context.

## 3. Normalization

Before aggregation, values must be normalized as applicable for:

- canonical instrument identity;
- side/direction;
- quantity/volume;
- notional value;
- account currency;
- valuation/conversion source and timestamp;
- leverage/margin basis;
- pending-order exposure;
- realized/unrealized loss.

Source-account identity must remain attached so aggregate exposure can be decomposed.

## 4. Shared Limits and Concentration

Policies may define shared limits for:

- aggregate notional exposure;
- aggregate quantity by instrument;
- net and gross directional exposure;
- aggregate margin/leverage exposure;
- aggregate loss/drawdown;
- concentration by instrument, strategy, broker, or platform;
- maximum simultaneously exposed accounts.

An individually valid request must be constrained or denied when it would cause the applicable cross-account aggregate to breach a shared limit.

## 5. Projected Exposure

Cross-account evaluation includes existing positions, pending orders, and the requested action.

```text
Existing Account-Set Exposure
            +
Pending Account-Set Exposure
            +
Requested Exposure
            ↓
Projected Cross-Account Exposure
            ↓
Shared / Concentration Limits
            ↓
Risk Arbitration
```

Individually valid concurrent requests must not collectively bypass a shared limit.

## 6. Concurrency and Reservation

Cross-account evaluation participates in shared-limit concurrency protection.

Reservations, serialization, or deterministic revalidation must protect overlapping account sets and identify the shared limit being protected.

A race that permits the aggregate to exceed a shared limit is a risk-control failure.

## 7. Staleness and Fail-Closed

Aggregation fails closed when mandatory account members, valuation inputs, exposure state, or conversion data are unavailable, stale beyond policy thresholds, contradictory, or unsafe to resolve.

An incomplete account set must not silently be treated as complete unless an explicit approved policy permits it.

Unknown/degraded aggregation state must be visible and may require `DENY`, `HALT`, or `SAFE` handling.

## 8. Audit Contract

Every material evaluation records:

- cross-account evaluation identifier;
- resolved account-set identifier/reference;
- scope/policy reference and version;
- aggregation method/version;
- normalization/conversion references and timestamps;
- shared limits evaluated;
- concentration metrics where applicable;
- projected aggregate exposure;
- reservation/concurrency state where applicable;
- stale/missing-data conditions;
- final risk decision and reason codes.

The aggregate decision must remain decomposable to source account exposures.

## 9. Governance Boundary

This contract is subordinate to the canonical Risk Management authority. It does not change the Risk Gate, Trading Engine lifecycle ownership, connector authority, ADR-006 binding, or database ownership.

No AI, strategy, portfolio aggregation service, adaptive runtime, or connector may convert this contract into an execution command or bypass Risk Manager.

## 10. Implementation Gate

Implementation is blocked until this contract is incorporated into the canonical Risk Management baseline and the resulting cross-document evidence is reviewed by the Architect/Project Owner.
